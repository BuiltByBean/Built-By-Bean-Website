The four totals at the top are lifetime, and answer a different question from the bars: hosting actually collected, read from paid Stripe invoices rather than from the fees agreed on the projects, against every Railway charge on record. Anything billed and not yet paid is called out separately rather than counted as earned.

The all-time line under each project asks the same thing about one client. Its collected figure is that client's, so a client with two builds shows the same total against both and says so. {%- if railway_since %} Its Railway figure starts at {{ railway_since.strftime('%B %Y') }}, which is when per-project figures began; every month before that was a single flat charge with no project on it, and is counted in the totals above but cannot be split between clients. {%- endif %}

The bars are one month, {{ month.strftime('%B %Y') }} - the last complete one. A project's cost is its mapped vendor charges plus anything filed by hand against it; a client-level cost with no project on it, like Stripe's fee, is not counted here, because that fee tracks whatever the client was invoiced for rather than what their application costs to run. The month in progress is left out, because a month three days old makes everything look cheap. A charge that buys a year, like a domain registration, is spread across the twelve months it covers rather than landing on the month it was invoiced; the expense ledger still keeps it whole, on its real date. The floor is {{ min_margin|format_currency }} left over after infrastructure, and it still has to cover the time spent keeping the thing alive.
