Every quarter

  1. Open Returns. Every return marked Due or Late shows exactly what to type.
  2. Sign in to eSystems with your one login, pick the business, then Sales and Use Tax, then File Original Return.
  3. Pick the period by its code: the year's last two digits and the quarter. 264 is October to December 2026.
  4. Credits: No. Customs broker refunds: No.
  5. Type the two numbers the return page gives for each location, in whole dollars, with taxable purchases 0. A location with no sales gets the No Sales or Purchases tick.
  6. Check the Review page's Total Tax Due against the return page, then pay by Electronic Check. A card adds about 2.3% that is not refunded.
  7. On the confirmation page press Print and save it as a PDF.
  8. Back on the return page, Record it: the reference number, payment reference and trace number, what the state charged, and the PDF.

Or open a Claude session and say "let's get my taxes paid". It reads this board, fills WebFile up to the payment, you pay, and it records the return here.

EntertainHQ's admin site shows the same returns: one marked filed there is recorded here, and one recorded here shows as filed there. The full research, with every rule's source, is the Texas sales tax playbook.

Dates and money

  • Quarterly returns are due the 20th of April, July, October and January. A weekend or federal holiday moves it to the next working day. WebFile takes a payment until 11:59 p.m. Central that day.
  • File every quarter, even with no sales. A missing return is late whatever it would have said.
  • On time and paid, you keep 0.5% of the tax as a timely filing discount.
  • Late is 5% of the tax for the first 30 days, 10% after, plus $50 per late return, plus interest from the 61st day.
  • A return that is not filed gets an Estimated Return, a guess set high on purpose: $1,158.93 and $2,202.53 where the real tax was $339.90 and $656.70. Filing the real return replaces it. Never pay off the estimate.

What counts

  • Software sold as a service is a data processing service in Texas: 20% of it is exempt, so taxable sales are 80% of total sales.
  • Total Texas Sales is everything billed, before tax. Tax billed back to a client is the state's money, not a sale.
  • A sale counts the day it is billed, not the day it is paid. If a client never pays, the tax on it comes back as a credit on a later return.
  • No deductions for expenses, unlike income tax. What lowers it: the 20% exemption, customers using it outside Texas, and buyers with an exemption certificate. Mark those clients exempt on the Sales tax page.
  • The rate is set by where you sell from: 6.25% state plus local. Fort Worth and Paris are both 2% local (Paris is 0.5% Lamar County and 1.5% city), so 8.25%.

Moving

  • Each place on the permit has a five digit WebFile location number. A sale belongs to the place you were selling from that day, so the quarter you move splits across two lines.
  • Add the new place, then tell the Comptroller the old one closed. Until you do, WebFile keeps a line for it every quarter, which gets the No Sales or Purchases tick.
  • Keep the dates right on the business's page here: the returns are split by them.

A new business

  1. Form it with the Secretary of State. That gives the SOS file number and an 11 digit Texas taxpayer number (look it up on the Comptroller's Franchise Tax Account Status search).
  2. Before the first sale, in eSystems choose Register for Texas Sales and Use Tax. One eSystems login covers every business you own. The owner types the SSN, EIN and driver's license. For software as a service: NAICS 541511, no tangible goods, each location with its start date and whether it is inside city limits.
  3. The permit and the sales tax WebFile number (RT and six digits) come by mail in two to three weeks, or the same day from 800-252-8880. Franchise tax has its own WebFile number, XT and six digits.
  4. In eSystems: Assign Tax/Fees, the taxpayer number, Sales and Use Tax, the RT number, Electronic Only, role Owner, tick the box, Assign, then Submit Changes.
  5. Here: Add a business with both WebFile numbers, the first taxable sale and every location with its WebFile location number. Its returns start appearing on their own.
  6. Charge the tax from the first invoice. Started selling first? File every back quarter at once, then ask for the penalty waiver.

Getting penalties back

  • Once every late return is filed and paid, ask for a waiver with Form 89-224, or by phone at 800-531-5441 ext. 3-4560.
  • It covers up to two late reports for a filer with no waiver in the last two years and good standing with the Secretary of State. "I didn't know" is not an exception to the tax itself, only to the penalties.
  • Paying the penalties first is fine: a granted waiver is refunded.
